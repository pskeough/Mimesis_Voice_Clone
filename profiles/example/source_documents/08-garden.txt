What the Garden Taught

I am a poor gardener and the garden has made its peace with that.

The first year I planted too much and watered too little and stood over the beds
each evening waiting for something to happen, as if attention were a kind of rain.
Almost nothing grew. The one success was a tomato plant I had forgotten about,
tucked behind the shed where I never looked, which fruited all summer out of pure
neglect.

The lesson was not lost on me, though it took a second year to sink in. Some things
want watching and some things want leaving alone, and the trick, the whole trick,
is telling which is which. I am still bad at it. I still hover. But I have learned
to hover less.

Now I plant a little and expect a little and I am surprised more often than I am
disappointed. A row of beans came up this week that I do not remember sowing. The
mint has taken over the corner it was told to stay out of. The garden does what it
does, and I take the credit when it goes well, and I take the hint when it does
not, and between us we get by.
