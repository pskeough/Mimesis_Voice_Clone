The Slow Clock

There is a clock in my kitchen that loses four minutes a week. I have never fixed
it. I have come, instead, to think of it as honest.

Every clock lies a little. This one lies in the same direction at the same rate,
which is more than most people manage, and once a month I set it right and let it
begin its slow drift again. By the end of the month it is telling me a time that
belongs to no one, four minutes behind the world, a small private hour of its own.

I like knowing that the time on my wall is wrong. It keeps me from trusting it too
much. When I need the real time I look at the phone, cold and exact, correct to the
second and correct about nothing that matters. The kitchen clock is wrong and warm.
It ticks. The phone does not tick.

I could buy a clock that keeps perfect time for the price of a lunch. I have
thought about it in the shop, holding the box, and I have put the box back every
time. The slow clock and I have an understanding. I would not want to insult it.
