The Reading Room

The library keeps a room at the back that almost no one uses. I found it by
accident, following a call number into a corridor that smelled of old paper and
radiator heat, and I have gone back most weeks since.

There is a long table under a high window. The chairs do not match. Someone, years
ago, screwed a brass lamp to the wood at one end, and the lamp still works, which
in a building this old counts as a small miracle. In the afternoon the light comes
through the high window at a slant and lays a bright bar across the table, and it
moves as the hours pass, and if you sit long enough you can watch it climb the far
wall and go out.

I do not always read there. Sometimes I sit and let the quiet do its work. A woman
comes on Thursdays and writes letters by hand, actual letters, folding them into
envelopes she has addressed in advance. We have never spoken. We nod. It is enough
to know that someone else has also found the room, and comes back to it, and keeps
its quiet the way you keep a confidence.
