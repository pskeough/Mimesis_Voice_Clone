Saturday Market

The market sets up in the dark and is half asleep when I get there. That is the
best hour, before the crowds, when the sellers are still stacking crates and the
prices have not yet hardened into the day.

An old woman sells apples from the back of a van. She does not weigh them. She
looks at you, decides something, and names a number, and the number is never the
same twice. I have watched her charge a smiling stranger more than she charged me,
and charge a tired man nothing at all, waving him off with a bag she pressed into
his hands. There is a justice in it that no scale could manage.

I buy what looks good and not what I planned to buy. A list is a way of deciding
yesterday what you should want today, and yesterday did not see these plums.

By nine the aisles are full and the quiet is gone and the good hour with it. I
carry my bag home along the emptying side streets while the market roars behind
me, and I unpack it on the kitchen table, and half of it is things I never meant
to bring home.
