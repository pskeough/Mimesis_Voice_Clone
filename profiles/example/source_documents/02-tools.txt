On Borrowed Tools

A neighbor lent me a plane last spring, an old wooden one with a blade worn to a
comma. I meant to keep it a week. I kept it a season.

The trouble with a good tool is that it teaches you what your own tools lack. I
had been fighting a door that would not close, shaving at it with a rasp, making
dust and no progress. The borrowed plane took a curl of wood off the edge in one
pass, clean, the shaving lifting and breaking like a small wave. The door shut.
It has shut every day since.

When I finally returned the plane I found I did not want to. That is the honest
part. I had grown used to reaching for the best thing in the drawer and finding
it there. My own plane sat beside it, dull, and I had learned to see it clearly
for the first time.

So I sharpened mine. It took an afternoon and a whetstone and more patience than
I usually keep on hand. The blade is not as good as his. It is good enough now,
and it is mine, and there is something in that worth the afternoon.
