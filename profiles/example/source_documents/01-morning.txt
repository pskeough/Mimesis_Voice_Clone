The Early Hour

I wake before the street does. The kettle goes on first, because the rest of the
morning waits on it, and while the water climbs toward a boil I stand at the
window and count the lit rooms across the road. Three tonight. A man in the
nearest one ties his shoes with the slow care of someone who has all the time in
the world, though I know he does not, because at seven he will run for the bus
like the rest of us.

The kettle ticks as it cools. I pour, and the steam fogs the lower pane, and for
a moment the street is gone behind it.

There is nothing remarkable about any of this. That is the point. The early hour
is the one part of the day that asks nothing, sells nothing, and forgives the
small failures of the day before. When the light finally comes up over the roofs
it finds me already awake, already halfway through the first cup, ready to be
disappointed by nothing in particular.
