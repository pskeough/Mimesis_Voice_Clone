Every unit, from the paper down to the sentence, makes one point and puts context before the new material. A paragraph opens with the thing it is about, carries its evidence, and closes on the claim that evidence supports. A sentence opens with what the reader already holds and closes on the new information that deserves weight. The action of a clause sits in its verb. The subject reaches its verb before anything long intervenes. A sentence is too long when it carries more emphasised items than it has closing positions, and not because it passed a word count.

The paper has one central contribution, and every section either sets it up, delivers it, or bounds it. Results are ordered by the logic of the argument, never by the order the work happened. A claim is stated at the strength the evidence carries, with the number, the uncertainty, and the N beside it. Speculation is labelled as speculation and kept out of the sentences that report what was measured. Where a gain has several possible sources, the source is isolated or the uncertainty is stated. Terms of art are used in their technical sense and no term borrows a colloquial connotation to do argumentative work. What remains open is named as open.
